How Australia's Food Exports Are Changing

Australia exports food and agricultural products worth tens of billions of dollars each year, with Asian markets playing an increasingly important role.

This visual story explores what Australia exports, where it goes, how these patterns have changed, and where Malaysia fits into the picture.

01

How large is Australia's food and agricultural export sector?

Australia's food and agricultural sector has expanded substantially over the past three decades, with international markets playing an important role in the sector's growth.

Agricultural production and exports over time

Agricultural exports have grown strongly alongside farm production. By 2024-25, export value reached about A$75.8 billion.

02

What does Australia export?

Australia's food and agricultural export basket contains a wide range of crop and livestock products, and the importance of these products has changed considerably over time.

Australia's agricultural export basket

Meat is the largest broad export category, contributing about 31% of Australia's agricultural export value in 2024-25.

Fisheries exports are excluded so the chart remains consistent with the agricultural export definition used throughout the visualisation.

Which commodities changed the most?

Most major commodity groups are worth substantially more than three decades ago. Wool is the only broad category shown with a lower export value in 2023-24 than in 1994-95.

How has the export mix changed over time?

Australia's export mix has shifted substantially over time as different crop and livestock categories have expanded at different rates.

03

Where do Australia's agricultural exports go?

Australian agricultural exports reach markets around the world, but demand is unevenly distributed and major destinations can change significantly over time.

Australia's major agricultural export markets

China is Australia's largest agricultural export destination, accounting for about 21% of export value in 2024-25. The five largest markets together account for roughly 53%.

How are export markets distributed across regions?

Australia's largest export markets are strongly concentrated across Asia, particularly East and Southeast Asia, while North America is also an important destination.

Regional totals represent only the destinations included among the selected top export markets rather than complete regional export totals.

Which export markets have risen or fallen in importance?

India recorded the sharpest recent rise, climbing from 22nd in 2019-20 to 6th in 2024-25 as exports increased from about A$371 million to A$2.8 billion.

04

Zooming into ASEAN and Malaysia

Southeast Asia is an important part of Australia's export network, but individual ASEAN markets differ in both the scale and mix of agricultural products they import.

What does Australia export across ASEAN?

ASEAN markets have noticeably different product mixes, showing that Australian agricultural exports serve different types of demand across the region.

What makes up Australia's agricultural exports to Malaysia?

Malaysia received about A$1.9 billion of Australian agricultural exports in 2024-25. Meat and live animals accounted for about 25%, grains and cereals 23%, and dairy and eggs 15%.

05

How dependent is Australian agriculture on exports?

Australia's reliance on international markets varies considerably across states and commodities, leaving some parts of the sector more export-oriented than others.

Which states are more export-oriented?

The value-based proxy suggests substantial differences across Australia, with several states and territories near three-quarters while Tasmania is closer to one-third.

This is a value-based proxy rather than a direct physical share of production exported. State-of-origin export data and production data represent different stages of the supply chain.

Which commodities rely most on export markets?

Some commodities depend heavily on overseas demand: around 85% of canola, 82% of mutton and lamb, 77% of beef and veal, and 75% of wheat production is exported.

Conclusion

Australia's food and agricultural sector is deeply connected to global markets

Export growth has been accompanied by major changes in product mix and destination markets. Asian economies now play a central role, while different states and commodities show varying levels of exposure to international demand.

Data sources: Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics and Sciences (ABARES), Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), and Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT).
